The condition

What is diabetes?

Diabetes is a chronic condition where blood sugar levels remain elevated beyond normal range. It occurs when the body can't produce enough insulin, or can't use the insulin it produces effectively. Left unmanaged over time, it can lead to serious complications including heart disease, kidney failure, and nerve damage.

The types

Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes

Type 1 diabetes

Typically diagnosed in children, teenagers, and young adults. The immune system mistakenly attacks the insulin-producing cells in the pancreas, making it impossible for the body to regulate blood sugar on its own. People living with Type 1 usually require daily insulin therapy and regular blood sugar monitoring.

Type 2 diabetes

The most common form, often diagnosed in adults over 40 — especially those who are overweight or inactive, though it's increasingly seen in younger people too. The body still produces insulin, but cells become resistant to it. Management usually includes lifestyle changes, medication, and sometimes insulin therapy.

Both types require careful long-term management to prevent complications such as heart disease, kidney problems, and nerve damage. Early detection and ongoing care can greatly improve quality of life.

Staying well

Ways to good health

Balanced diet

Whole grains, fruit, vegetables and lean protein help regulate blood sugar and support overall health.

Regular activity

Walking, cycling or swimming improves insulin sensitivity and lowers the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes.

Sleep and hydration

Enough sleep and water intake plays a real role in regulating blood sugar and supporting the immune system.

Regular monitoring

Checking blood sugar, managing stress and taking medication as prescribed improves long-term outcomes.
